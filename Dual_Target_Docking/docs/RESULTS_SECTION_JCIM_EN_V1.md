# Results (JCIM Articles draft, English)

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Experimental data supply limits strict dual-target benchmark construction

To determine whether public bioactivity data can support a strict dual-target recognition evaluation, we first audited ligand supply for 49 ChEMBL-cached candidate target pairs (Figure 2). Ligands that meet the activity threshold on one target and are explicitly inactive on the other are directional selective hard negatives.

Under the strict labeling rule (dual: both ends pChEMBL ≥ 6.5; selective: active end ≥ 6.5 and opposite end ≤ 5.5), pairs that simultaneously supply enough A-only and B-only hard negatives were scarce. Only four pairs met the thick-panel gate of ≥50 strict hard negatives on **both** ends. After excluding metal-dependent HDAC1/HDAC6, PIK3CA/mTOR, AChE/BChE, and PIK3CA/PIK3CB formed three relatively well-supplied pairs. EGFR/HER2 retained only 7 strict B-only ligands and was therefore kept as a supply-limited case (Table 1). A zero-docking recount in BindingDB and PubChem supported the same scarcity conclusion (Table S12).

The size of the final benchmark was therefore constrained by the availability of experimentally defined directional hard negatives. The strict 6.5/5.5 rule quantifies supply and records panel construction, whereas θ = 6.0 defines the experimental-state labels for all primary AUROCs (Methods 2.1). Recounting the same 49 unique pairs at the primary θ = 6.0 rule, without the 5.5–6.5 gray-zone exclusion, found 17 pairs with dual, A-only, and B-only each n ≥ 10, and the same 17 also had neither n ≥ 10 (Table S44; Figure S7A). Sixteen of those 17 are non-metal. Docking evaluation remains the original four pairs; the census is a label-supply result, not a 17-pair docking benchmark.

### 3.2 Benchmark formulation changes apparent dual-target discrimination

On the frozen four pairs, AutoDock Vina scores were evaluated under one unified θ = 6.0 label rule using pocket-matched directional AUROC (Figure 1B; Methods 2.4). Scores are \(S=-E_{\mathrm{Vina}}\) (higher better); dual is the positive class. The prespecified worst-direction discrimination summary is `summary_min`. Pair ranking is unchanged under arithmetic, geometric, and harmonic means (Table S26). For AChE/BChE and PIK3CA/PIK3CB, θ = 6.0 gives the same classification and AUROC as the stricter construction gate (Table S4). EGFR/HER2 and PIK3CA/mTOR become underpowered on B_only under the strict rule. Ranking trends held across the full threshold grid (Figure S1A).

EGFR/HER2, AChE/BChE, PIK3CA/PIK3CB, and PIK3CA/mTOR gave directional `summary_min` values of 0.430, 0.606, 0.500, and 0.692, respectively (Table 2; Figure 4A). Dual-versus-B-only AUROC is 0.430 on EGFR/HER2 and 0.500 on PIK3CA/PIK3CB, whereas PIK3CA/mTOR reaches 0.714 and 0.692 on the two directions. Relative to pooling, pocket matching raised point estimates without changing rank order (Table S6).

The same frozen scores were then scored under Dual versus neither and Dual versus all non-duals (Table 3; Figure 3). Dual versus neither is a descriptive comparator that uses experimental inactives (`vina_mean`). EGFR/HER2 provides the clearest formulation example. Dual versus neither yielded AUROC 0.756 [0.562, 0.920] (n_neg = 12), whereas directional `summary_min` remained 0.430 [0.282, 0.578]. Dual versus all non-duals fell to 0.551 [0.443, 0.666], indicating that introducing selective ligands made the discrimination task substantially harder. In a mixed-library ranking of all 110 EGFR/HER2 ligands by `vina_mean`, the Top-10 contained 1 dual, 5 A-only, 4 B-only, and 0 neither (EF10 = 0.393; hard-negative fraction = 0.90); EF5 was also below random (Table S25). AChE/BChE and PIK3CA/PIK3CB showed only small Dual-versus-neither increments (0.649 and 0.559) whose intervals overlap the directional arms. PIK3CA/mTOR Dual versus neither is underpowered (neither n = 4); Dual versus all non-duals on that pair was 0.674, close to `summary_min` 0.692.

Because the comparison above changes both negative composition and score aggregation, we repeated it with the score held fixed (Table S34). On EGFR/HER2 pocket A, Dual versus B-only was 0.430 and Dual versus neither was 0.808; the descriptive difference was 0.378 [0.205, 0.547]. On pocket B, the corresponding difference was 0.054 [−0.157, 0.246]. Thus, the EGFR/HER2 weak arm reflects negative-class composition under the same pocket score rather than mean-score aggregation alone. Of the other six pair-direction contrasts, five intervals included zero; the PIK3CA/PIK3CB pocket-A difference was 0.159 [0.001, 0.321]. These exploratory intervals were not multiplicity adjusted, and PIK3CA/mTOR remained underpowered with four neither compounds.

All four 95% bootstrap intervals for `summary_min` included 0.5; thus, at the present sample sizes, no target pair yielded clear evidence excluding chance-level discrimination. A simulation-based detectable-effect analysis at the observed class sizes showed that these samples resolve large directional effects more readily than moderate ones. When both arms had true AUROC 0.70, the probability that the `summary_min` CI excluded 0.5 was 0.62, 0.50, 0.56, and 0.22 for EGFR/HER2, AChE/BChE, PIK3CA/PIK3CB, and PIK3CA/mTOR; at true AUROC 0.60 the same probabilities were 0.03–0.07 (Table S31; Figure S6). Failure to exclude 0.5 therefore does not establish equivalence to chance.

Both-end scores were obtained for 110/110 EGFR/HER2 ligands, 95/100 AChE/BChE ligands, 99/100 PIK3CA/PIK3CB ligands, and 48/48 PIK3CA/mTOR ligands (Table S27). The six main-panel failures were large or flexible (48–85 heavy atoms; 17–29 rotatable bonds); four were deliberately skipped by the protocol at high torsional freedom and two timed out. Missingness is therefore chemical-coverage dependent, not random. On AChE/BChE, rank-extreme lower bounds were 0.560 and 0.584 for the two directional arms, compared with complete-case estimates 0.650 and 0.606. On PIK3CA/PIK3CB, using the available pocket-B score for the A-only PAB_034 changed that arm from 0.6905 to 0.6952 and left `summary_min` at 0.500. Alternative scorers on the same Vina poses did not change the overall ranking (Tables S14–S15; Figure S1B). Independent pose generation is reported in Results 3.7.

### 3.3 Ligand properties and chemotype explain much of the apparent signal

Pocket-matched docking was first compared with four prespecified physicochemical descriptors (Figure 4B; Table 2). Relative to the best single-descriptor reference on each pair, the paired difference in summary_min was −0.052, −0.128, −0.122, and +0.229 for EGFR/HER2, AChE/BChE, PIK3CA/PIK3CB, and PIK3CA/mTOR; all four 95% confidence intervals include 0 (Table S19; Figure S3C). Even the largest positive point difference, on PIK3CA/mTOR, is therefore not distinguishable from the ligand-property reference with the present sample.

AChE/BChE is a direct confounding case. Mean TPSA was ≈ 75 for dual ligands versus ≈ 51 for selective hard negatives (Figure 4C). TPSA alone gave AUROC ≈ 0.769, above Vina under the same contrast (≈ 0.56). Adding heavy-atom count and TPSA raised dual-versus-B-only AUROC from 0.606 to 0.807, while the docking-score odds ratio was only ≈ 1.18 (Figure 7C). Thus, part of the apparent docking discrimination on this arm can be accounted for by ligand physicochemical information.

PIK3CA/mTOR differs in degree. Adding heavy-atom count and TPSA shifted AUROC by about +0.07 to +0.11, with docking odds ratios ≈ 2.19 and 3.08. The paired difference versus the descriptor baseline still includes 0. After ligand-efficiency normalization, only PIK3CA/mTOR remained above the heavy-atom baseline (0.657 versus 0.463).

A two-dimensional chemical baseline makes the same point (Figure 7A). ECFP4 logistic regression under Bemis–Murcko scaffold GroupKFold yielded fold AUROCs of about 0.78–0.91 on several arms, well above the corresponding docking contrasts — for example 0.89 versus 0.43 for EGFR/HER2 dual-versus-B-only. The experimental labels therefore contain ligand-structure-associated information that can be exploited without receptor information. On PIK3CA/mTOR, \(n_{\mathrm{scaffolds}} \approx n\), so the split is nearly leave-one-scaffold. A random `StratifiedKFold` check sits on average +0.026 above the scaffold split across eight directional contrasts (Table S20; Figure S3D).

The expanded class-wise audit confirmed broad chemical-space imbalance rather than a single-descriptor exception (Table S38). For AChE/BChE, median TPSA was 76.0 Å² in dual ligands versus 38.8 and 48.1 Å² in A-only and B-only ligands; median molecular weight was 471.6 versus 409.5 and 425.6 Da. Median nearest-dual ECFP4 similarity for these selective classes was only 0.236 and 0.253. Across the four pairs, most class-specific scaffolds were singletons, and several neither classes were especially distant from duals (median nearest-dual similarity 0.154 for EGFR/HER2 and 0.177 for PIK3CA/PIK3CB). These post-hoc summaries reinforce chemical-composition confounding but do not apportion how much of any AUROC is causal receptor recognition.

Under the present scaffold-grouped benchmark, the largest absolute change after adding the pocket-matched docking score to ECFP4 was 0.020 (unrounded −0.0198 on PIK3CA/mTOR dual versus A-only), and several changes were negative (Table S24). At T ≥ 0.3, PIK3CA/PIK3CB dual versus A-only fell from 0.691 to 0.503 (n_neg = 11), whereas distant hard negatives (T < 0.3) yielded 0.819; T ≥ 0.4/0.5 cells were often n_neg ≤ 7, and T ≥ 0.7 was empty (Table S23). On potency- or size-matched subsets, dual-versus-B-only remained weak or near chance on EGFR/HER2 and PIK3CA/PIK3CB (about 0.45–0.52), with per-arm n often < 15 (Table S5; Figure 7D).

### 3.4 Activity aggregation, ligand panels, and receptor realization affect evaluation outcomes

Primary labels use the maximum available pChEMBL value. Replacing the maximum with the median of repeated measurements changed 7/110, 1/95, 1/99, and 0/48 ligand-state assignments (agreements 93.6%, 98.9%, 99.0%, and 100%). Pair-level `summary_min` on the API-refetched labels moved only modestly (0.417→0.424, 0.606→0.629, 0.500→0.500, and 0.692→0.692; Table S29).

Measurement frequency was nevertheless class-imbalanced (Table S35). On PIK3CA/mTOR, the median total number of API activity records across both targets was 22 for dual ligands, versus 3, 3, and 2 for A-only, B-only, and neither; the dual interquartile range was 10.75–34. EGFR/HER2 contained a dual ligand with 318 records, and AChE/BChE a dual ligand with 207. These counts do not show that measurement frequency caused the observed AUROCs, but they demonstrate that max-versus-median label stability does not eliminate profiling-intensity and literature-selection differences among classes.

The post-hoc high-confidence current-ChEMBL view audited 2748 activity records and retained 1546 after the explicit target, confidence, relation, endpoint, validity, and duplicate filters; 513 records were excluded as potential duplicates (Table S36). All 352 scored ligands retained at least one qualifying record at each target, and all 352 high-confidence θ = 6.0 states matched the frozen states. Consequently, the four directional point estimates were unchanged. This result addresses the specified record-level filters on the current database snapshot, but not assay-condition equivalence or selection into the dual-measured subset.

The coverage audit quantified that selection (Table S37). Among structures with a usable pChEMBL value at at least one member of a pair, only 14.5%, 34.0%, 23.3%, and 26.5% had usable values at both targets for EGFR/HER2, AChE/BChE, PIK3CA/PIK3CB, and PIK3CA/mTOR, respectively. These proportions are not prevalence estimates because missing values were not treated as inactive. Document concentration also differed by class. Most prominently, all four PIK3CA/mTOR neither ligands and all eight retained activity records for that class came from one ChEMBL document; results involving that negative class are therefore inseparable from a small, single-document sample.

Document-blocked cross-validation on the same frozen Vina scores left the EGFR/HER2 directional weak arm unchanged at 0.430 (document-cluster bootstrap 95% CI [0.321, 0.617]; 5 valid folds; 23 groups; 113 documents; Table S39). The corresponding Dual-versus-A-only arm remained 0.666. Under the same folds, ECFP4 logistic OOF AUROC on EGFR/HER2 Dual versus B-only was 0.623, lower than the scaffold-grouped 0.89 baseline, showing that same-paper series contributed to the two-dimensional signal. Seven of eight directional arms were estimable; PIK3CA/mTOR Dual versus B-only had only one fold with both classes among nine document-connected groups (largest group 19/30 ligands) and is reported as not stably estimable without regrouping (Table S40).

The pre-frozen 2018 time split did not meet the sample gate on any pair (test counts: EGFR/HER2 6/3/14/2; AChE/BChE 8/5/15/6; PIK3CA/PIK3CB 12/11/0/3; PIK3CA/mTOR 2/0/1/0 for dual / A-only / B-only / neither; Table S41). The 2015 sensitivity left only AChE/BChE above n = 10 in all three directional classes (11/11/24). Because fewer than two pairs were evaluable at the primary cutoff, the split is not packaged as external validation. BindingDB both-end equal-relation ligands remaining after scored-panel InChIKey removal still had dual/A-only/B-only each n ≥ 10 on all four pairs (EGFR/HER2 1589/161/62; AChE/BChE 966/450/230; PIK3CA/PIK3CB 1341/261/257; PIK3CA/mTOR 2008/238/266; Table S43). UniChem ChEMBL-map overlap and complete panel-PMID lookup were not finished, so those ligands are not claimed as database-external. They were not docked and are not packaged as external validation.

A machine assay-context audit flagged 186 priority ligands (1163 retained activity rows), including 98 EGFR/HER2 directional ligands, 40 mixed-endpoint ligands, 22 with both biochemical and functional assays, and the four PIK3CA/mTOR neither ligands (Table S42). A metadata review then assigned 179 include / 7 uncertain / 0 exclude; the seven uncertain ligands retained their frozen classes. Protein construct and mutation status remain unknown because assay free-text was unavailable. Frozen labels were not changed.

Lowering exhaustiveness from 16 to 8 moved PIK3CA/mTOR summary_min from 0.692 to 0.660 (Figure S1D). On PM110 (n_scored = 115; 30 / 30 / 30 dual / A_only / B_only), Vina summary_min was 0.648 [0.51, 0.76], about 0.04 below PM48, with the same ranking trend (Figure S1C). On the unused-pool holdout (20 / 20 / 20 per pair; seed 20260731; EGFR/HER2 not eligible), PIK3CA/mTOR summary_min was 0.765 [0.603, 0.891], AChE/BChE was 0.618 [0.422, 0.759], and PIK3CA/PIK3CB fell to 0.425 [0.241, 0.618] (Tables S8, S16). The holdout shares the same ChEMBL extraction batch.

We next tested receptor realization, holding one pocket frozen and replacing the other (Figure 5; Tables S9, S30). Three alternate crystals passed cognate redocking QC (best-of-9 RMSD 0.607 Å for 4JPS, 0.624 Å for 5DXT, and 0.515 Å for 4JSX). On PIK3CA/mTOR, replacing PIK3CA 4L23 with 4JPS or 5DXT, while holding mTOR at 4JT6, dropped PM48 summary_min from 0.692 to 0.486 [0.259, 0.692] and 0.505 [0.292, 0.696] (Figure 5A). The change concentrated in the D/B direction that depends on the alternate PIK3CA structure; D/A stayed at 0.714. Replacing mTOR 4JT6 with 4JSX gave summary_min 0.639 [0.418, 0.776].

The same PIK3CA crystals on the PIK3CA/PIK3CB panel, with 2WXF frozen, **raised** summary_min from 0.500 to 0.691 [0.516, 0.779] (4JPS) and 0.685 [0.506, 0.768] (5DXT) (Figure 5B). Dual versus A-only stayed at 0.691; dual versus B-only rose from 0.500 to 0.707 and 0.685. All three PIK3CA conditions used the same 99 scored ligands (Table S30). Thus, replacing the same PIK3CA receptor altered the apparent discrimination in opposite directions across the two target pairs.

### 3.5 Wrong-pocket controls reveal an unresolved out-of-panel failure

On the main panels, pocket-matched summary_min exceeded the wrong-pocket control on all four pairs; matched-minus-wrong differences were 0.170, 0.161, 0.151, and 0.090. The EGFR/HER2 and AChE/BChE intervals exclude 0; the PIK3CA/PIK3CB and PIK3CA/mTOR intervals include 0 (Tables S6, S17; Figure 6A; Figure S3A). Wrong-pocket summary_min values were 0.260, 0.444, 0.349, and 0.602.

The point-estimate relationship reversed on the unused-pool holdout (Figure 6B). Wrong-pocket summary_min was 0.788, 0.643, and 0.520 for PIK3CA/mTOR, AChE/BChE, and PIK3CA/PIK3CB, versus matched-pocket 0.765, 0.618, and 0.425. All three matched-minus-wrong point differences were negative (−0.023 / −0.025 / −0.095), and every 95% confidence interval included 0 (Table S17; Figure S3B). After potency or size matching, wrong-pocket remained ≥ matched-pocket (Table S13). Scoring-independent contact_count reached AUROC 0.698–0.714 on the B direction, but this coarse surrogate cannot account for the magnitude of the observed Vina wrong-pocket discrimination (Figure 6D; Table S11). The holdout wrong-pocket reversal is therefore an unresolved failure mode exposed by the benchmark.

### 3.6 Exploratory structural context

Structural similarity did not predict screening discrimination: 5DXT and 4L23 showed low pocket-local Cα RMSD (0.343 Å), yet their benchmark performance differed substantially (Table S10). A geometric occupancy snapshot (heavy-atom proximity ≤ 4.5 Å to 20 frozen PIK3CA pocket residues on the PM48 poses) showed the largest occupancy shifts at Met772, Leu807, Gln859, Thr856, Cys838, Glu849, Phe930, and Asp933 (Table S33). The performance shift coincided with these altered contact patterns, providing a structural hypothesis for the receptor sensitivity. Occupancy changes do not establish that any residue caused the AUROC change, and they do not explain the opposite PIK3CA/PIK3CB shift. Pose typology is retained in the Supporting Information (Table S7; Note S1).

### 3.7 Independent pose generation leaves the EGFR/HER2 formulation gap intact

GNINA CNN and RTMScore in Results 3.2 rescored frozen Vina poses. To test whether the formulation effect depends on that pose-generation engine, GNINA 1.3.2 was run in docking-search mode on the same frozen EGFR/HER2 and PIK3CA/mTOR ligands, receptors, boxes, and exhaustiveness (Methods 2.3; Table S32). Analyses used ligands with complete both-end scores (EGFR/HER2 n = 109 after neither ligand EH120_109 failed both pockets; PIK3CA/mTOR n = 47 after A-only ligand PM48_19 failed both pockets). Directional n_scored remained 28 / 38 / 32 on EGFR/HER2 and became 18 / 13 / 12 on PIK3CA/mTOR.

On EGFR/HER2, Dual versus neither remained high (AUROC 0.783 [0.610, 0.922]; n_neg = 11), whereas directional `summary_min` was 0.220 [0.109, 0.343]. The mixed-library Top-10 by mean pocket score again contained 1 dual and 9 experimental selectives (4 A-only, 5 B-only; EF10 = 0.389). The formulation effect is therefore not Vina-specific under this independent pose-generation protocol. On PIK3CA/mTOR, directional `summary_min` stayed the same sign (0.633 versus Vina 0.692). This comparison tests persistence of the formulation gap; it is not a claim that GNINA outperforms or underperforms Vina.

### 3.8 Label-supply scale, property matching, and AND filters

The θ = 6.0 census (Results 3.1; Table S44) shows that experimentally defined four-state labels exist beyond the four docked pairs. It does not show that docking was evaluated on those extra pairs.

On the frozen scored panels, 1:1 property matching at a 1.0 SD caliper left Dual-versus-B-only AUROC near chance where the cell was powered: EGFR/HER2 0.566 [0.356, 0.781] (n = 16), AChE/BChE 0.462 [0.243, 0.692] (n = 13), and PIK3CA/PIK3CB 0.556 [0.284, 0.827] (n = 9). The 0.5 SD caliper and the PIK3CA/mTOR 1.0 SD Dual-versus-B-only cell (n = 5) were underpowered (Table S45). Property matching is a DUDE-Z-style control that uses experimental selectives rather than generated decoys; it is not causal adjustment.

An AND-like dual filter on Dual+A-only+B-only libraries makes the same point as mixed-library Top-10 (Table S25) at explicit operating points (Table S46; Figure S7B). On EGFR/HER2, the median Dual `vina_worst` cut retained 14/28 Dual ligands but 33 selectives (precision 0.298; hard-negative fraction 0.702). Tightening to the Dual 90th percentile lowered Dual precision further to 0.130 because B-only ligands dominated the extreme AND scores. AChE/BChE precision rose to 0.600 at that tail (n_pass = 5). These cuts are diagnostics for dual-pocket filters used in virtual screening and generative design, not a claim that any named generator was re-scored.

Ligand-only models on the full ChEMBL maps, not the docked n ≈ 28 panels, recovered Dual versus neither more easily than Dual versus selectives on every pair (Table S47; Figure S7C). ECFP4 GroupKFold Dual-versus-neither AUROCs were 0.921, 0.851, 0.835, and 0.956 for EGFR/HER2, AChE/BChE, PIK3CA/PIK3CB, and PIK3CA/mTOR, versus directional ECFP4 `summary_min` 0.801, 0.744, 0.720, and 0.887. Dual versus B-only remained high in two-dimensional chemistry (EGFR/HER2 0.864) even though docking on the sampled panel was 0.430. The experimental labels are therefore chemically structured at map scale; docking is not thereby shown to recover that structure on the hard arm. Table S47 does not replace Table 2.
