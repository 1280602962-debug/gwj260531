# Discussion (English working draft)

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Benchmark formulation changes the evidentiary standard

The primary question is whether changing the control class, and the compound set that class brings with it, changes apparent dual-target docking evidence. Task differences are pair- and direction-dependent. On frozen EGFR/HER2 and JAK1/TYK2 scores, Dual versus neither looked substantially stronger than the corresponding directional selectivity comparisons (Results 3.2; Table 3). Independent GNINA pose generation reproduced both gaps, so the observation is not Vina-specific here. The EGFR/HER2 formulation gap remained positive across all five seeds (Table S9). JAK1/TYK2 is a second formulation-gap pair (0.770 versus 0.365; independent GNINA 0.705 versus 0.317). Holding the score channel fixed isolates aggregation, but it does not isolate biology: replacing B-only with neither also replaces the molecules. The remaining pairs did not support a universal effect, and the eight rows are not averaged. Relative to Zhou et al.,[9] “two-pocket scoring does not guarantee true dual inhibitors” is not itself new. The distinction here is three evaluation-design steps: splitting the two selective states into directional tasks; holding the score channel fixed while quantifying the change in control class; and using ligand-only and pocket-correspondence controls to bound how far the remaining signal can be explained. Existing docking benchmarks have shown that decoy construction and chemical bias change virtual-screening interpretation;[5–7,12,13,21] the same concern applies when dual-target conclusions depend on the experimental negative class. Wu et al. demonstrated that large-library docking can prospectively yield joint binders;[19] DualFourClass addresses a different question—whether retrospective evidence for dual-target recognition changes when experimentally selective ligands rather than nonbinders define the negative class. Kinase-Bench tests selective enrichment against kinase-specific decoys,[22] whereas DualFourClass constructs four experimentally measured states on each pair.

### 4.2 Ligand chemistry and receptor realization

Physicochemical descriptors and chemotype already carry much of the experimental-label information. Adding a pocket-matched docking score to scaffold-grouped ECFP4 changed AUROC by at most 0.023 (Results 3.3; Table S5). That small increment does not imply that all structural information is uninformative. Without ligand-level controls, an apparently strong dual-target docking result may only recover molecular properties associated with the dual label.[7,12]

Apparent discrimination also depends on receptor realization. Holding mTOR fixed and replacing PIK3CA with alternate crystals lowered discrimination on PIK3CA/mTOR (Results 3.4; Figure 4B), consistent with kinase cross-docking work that treats receptor representation as a performance variable.[14] On the single receptor-verified pair available for this test, substitution only lowered apparent discrimination.

### 4.3 Implications for dual-target virtual screening

Favorable scores in both pockets do not automatically establish experimentally defined dual activity. Discrimination against neither-class ligands can hide a failure to recognize single-target actives. After a dual-pocket score looks favorable, four practical checks remain: (i) directional discrimination against A-only and B-only single-target-selective controls; (ii) whether a ligand-only ECFP or property model recovers a similar signal under a leak-resistant split; (iii) an unused ligand pool or document-blocked split; (iv) at least one alternate receptor realization (Figure 1; TOC graphic). Figure 5 reports matched minus mismatched `summary_min`. A holdout CI that includes 0 does not prove loss of pocket advantage, and a significant main-panel result with a nonsignificant holdout does not establish that the two Δ values differ. On homologous pairs, a mismatched pocket can still produce correlated scores, so pocket swap is a diagnostic rather than a decisive test of structural specificity. The supported conclusion is that a stable matched-pocket advantage was not obtained. POLYGON and related generative methods show that dual-target design can be experimentally validated,[20] but docking-based dual claims still need selectivity-aware evaluation before two-pocket occupancy is treated as recognition evidence. This concern is consistent with JCIM work showing that docking rescoring performance varies across experimentally grounded screening sets.[15]

### 4.4 Limitations

The analysis covers eight target pairs rather than a target-general benchmark. Bidirectional selective supply differs substantially across pairs, so candidate-pool rules and some sampling constraints are not fully uniform. Primary statistics use unified θ = 6.0 labels, and threshold and unused-ligand holdout analyses probe that choice, but panel-construction differences cannot be ruled out as a contributor to cross-pair comparisons. JAK1 and PPARA are each reused, and several pairs belong to related families, so the eight rows are not eight statistically independent replicates and probe consistency across systems rather than a large independent sample. PPARG/PPARA is the only main-panel worst-arm CI entirely above 0.5, and that number is membership-sensitive (unused-pool holdout) and formulation-sensitive (RTM). Labels are derived from heterogeneous public bioactivity records and require complete measurements at both targets (complete-case fractions 14.5%–34.0%). Protonation, tautomer, and undefined-stereochemistry ensembles were not systematically enumerated, so low directional performance cannot be attributed solely to the scoring function. Receptor dependence was evaluated only for selected structures, and no prospective experimental validation of newly predicted dual ligands was performed. No target pair met the prespecified BindingDB external-slice gate, so the slice is not claimed as external validation. Cognate best-of-nine QC establishes search coverage, not a top-ranked-pose validation. MCL1/Bcl-xL was formally demoted to Supporting Information.
