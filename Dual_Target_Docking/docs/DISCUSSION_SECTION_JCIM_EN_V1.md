# Discussion (JCIM Articles draft, English)

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Benchmark formulation changes the evidentiary standard for dual-target docking

The benchmark operationalizes dual-target recognition as discrimination against experimentally defined A-selective and B-selective ligands in both directions. On the same frozen EGFR/HER2 scores, Dual versus neither gave AUROC 0.756, whereas directional `summary_min` was 0.430 (Results 3.2; Table 3). Holding the score fixed on pocket A retained a difference of 0.378 [0.205, 0.547] between neither and B-only negatives (Table S34). A dual-versus-inactive formulation can therefore present a more favorable picture than the directional hard-negative task in this case; the remaining pair-directions do not support a universal effect. Under an independent GNINA pose-generation protocol, Dual versus neither remained 0.783 and directional `summary_min` 0.220, so the EGFR/HER2 case finding is not Vina-specific here (Results 3.7; Table S32).

Relative to Zhou et al.,[9] the present comparison asks whether docking separates dual-actives from experimentally defined selectives, not only from inactives. Existing docking benchmarks have shown that decoy construction, chemical bias, and assay-derived labels change virtual-screening interpretation;[5–7,12,13] the same concern applies here, because dual-target conclusions depend on how the negative class is defined experimentally. Property-matched experimental selectives (Table S45) and Dual-percentile AND filters (Table S46) are the dual-target analogues of that decoy-bias lesson: on EGFR/HER2, requiring both pockets to look Dual-like preferentially retained B-only ligands.

The four-state label question is not confined to the docked panels. Seventeen unique ChEMBL pairs in the frozen 49-pair list admit Dual/A-only/B-only n ≥ 10 at θ = 6.0 (Table S44). Ligand-only ECFP4 on the full maps of the four docked pairs still finds Dual versus neither easier than Dual versus selectives (Table S47). That is a chemical-label result. It does not license a 17-pair docking claim.

### 4.2 Apparent docking signal cannot be attributed to docking scores alone

Physicochemical descriptors and chemotype already carry much of the experimental-label information. On AChE/BChE, TPSA alone discriminated better than docking, and adding heavy-atom count and TPSA raised dual-versus-B-only AUROC while the docking-score odds ratio remained near one (Results 3.3). Scaffold-grouped ECFP4 exceeded docking on several arms—for example 0.89 versus 0.43 on EGFR/HER2 dual-versus-B-only—so the labels contain ligand-structure-associated information that can be used without receptor information. Adding the pocket-matched docking score to ECFP4 changed CV AUROC by at most 0.020 in absolute value. At T ≥ 0.3, the strongest unmatched arm (PIK3CA/PIK3CB dual versus A-only) fell from 0.691 to 0.503, whereas distant hard negatives (T < 0.3) rose to 0.819. Without these ligand-level controls, an apparently strong dual-target docking result may only be recovering molecular properties associated with the dual label.[7,12]

The broader class audit is consistent with that interpretation: descriptor medians, scaffold composition, and proximity to the dual class differ across experimental states (Table S38). These are not nuisance variables that can be assumed away after a single covariate adjustment. Conversely, chemical imbalance does not prove that docking contains no receptor-dependent information; it limits attribution of the observed rank discrimination to pocket recognition.

### 4.3 Receptor realization is another important dimension of the evaluation condition and can raise or lower apparent discrimination

Apparent discrimination also depends on how the evaluation condition is specified. Replacing maximum pChEMBL with the median of repeated measurements left pair-level conclusions essentially unchanged (Results 3.4). An unused-pool holdout from the same ChEMBL harvest preserved some ranking trends and reversed others. Holding one pocket frozen and replacing the other raised apparent discrimination on one PIK3CA-related pair and lowered it on the other (Figure 5). These receptor-realization changes are consistent with kinase cross-docking work that treats receptor representation as a performance variable.[14] A geometric occupancy snapshot on the PM48 PIK3CA poses coincided with contact-pattern shifts at residues including Met772 and Leu807 (Table S33). That pattern is a structural hypothesis, not evidence that any residue caused the AUROC change, and it does not explain the opposite PIK3CA/PIK3CB shift.

### 4.4 Implications for dual-target virtual screening and generative design

These results have direct methodological implications for dual-target virtual screening and may also apply to generative design workflows that use docking as a downstream filter. Favorable scores in both pockets do not automatically establish experimentally defined dual activity. On EGFR/HER2, an AND cut at the median Dual `vina_worst` had Dual precision 0.298 (Table S46). This concern is consistent with recent JCIM studies showing that docking rescoring performance can vary substantially across experimentally grounded screening sets.[15] In the present dual-target task, docking scores therefore need to be read together with experimentally defined selective hard negatives and ligand-only chemical controls.

The same four checks can be applied as a practical diagnostic (Figure 8). After a dual-pocket score looks favorable: (i) require directional discrimination against A-only and B-only hard negatives; (ii) ask whether a ligand-only ECFP or property model recovers a similar signal under a leak-resistant split; (iii) test an unused ligand pool and, where documents exist, a document-blocked split; (iv) replace at least one receptor realization. A failure at any step marks the claim as formulation-, chemistry-, panel-, or receptor-dependent computational evidence.

### 4.5 Limitations

First, the benchmark contains only four target pairs because experimentally defined dual-target hard negatives are scarce under the strict construction gate. At the primary θ = 6.0 rule, 17 unique pairs in the 49-pair audit have Dual/A-only/B-only n ≥ 10, but those extra pairs were not added to Table 2. MCL1/Bcl-xL was docked only as an applicability stress-test because the stricter LC6 pose-gold requirement was not validly completed and is not a fifth evaluation pair. Two Table 2 pairs share PIK3CA and three involve kinase ATP sites; K = 4 is therefore a data-constrained docking case panel rather than a representative or comprehensive dual-target suite. The four `summary_min` values also mix panel-construction differences (strict 6.5/5.5 versus θ = 6.0; unequal n and scaffold caps), receptor choices, and target-pair biology. They should not be interpreted as a target ranking. Current class sizes resolve large directional effects more readily than moderate ones, and all four primary intervals include 0.5 (Table S31).

Second, the operational activity states are ChEMBL-derived and require a usable measurement at both targets. This complete-case restriction can select well-profiled compounds, known polypharmacology, and study-specific chemical series. The unused-pool resample remains within the same extraction batch, target pairs, labeling rules, and receptor protocols; it tests panel-membership dependence but is neither temporal nor database-external validation. BindingDB/PubChem checks in Table S12 were count-level only. Table S43 remains the historical REST supply count after scored-panel InChIKey removal. A BindingDB-native 202608 archive rebuild then applied literature, structure, and ECFP4 < 0.70 filters and yielded zero pairs meeting the pre-frozen primary external gate (Tables S48–S49). Remaining n are upper bounds because ChEMBL document lookup was partial; even as upper bounds the slice was not packaged as external evaluation and was not docked.

The coverage audit makes the complete-case limitation substantial rather than hypothetical: only 14.5%–34.0% of structures with a usable value at either target had usable values at both. The activity of the remaining singly measured structures is unknown. Source-document concentration further limits independence, most severely for the four-member PIK3CA/mTOR neither class, which came from one document. Document-blocked CV on the frozen panels did not reverse the EGFR/HER2 weak directional arm, and it showed that PIK3CA/mTOR Dual versus B-only cannot be stably estimated once shared documents define groups. A pre-frozen 2018 literature-year split did not yield two evaluable pairs, so temporal external validation is not claimed.

Third, assay heterogeneity remains. IC50, Ki, Kd, EC50, and Potency values from non-equivalent experimental systems were mapped to one threshold. Primary curation uses maximum pChEMBL, which can depend on measurement frequency. The median sensitivity addresses aggregation only, and Table S35 shows substantial class-wise profiling imbalance, particularly for PIK3CA/mTOR dual ligands. A post-hoc current-ChEMBL rebuild using human single-protein, confidence≥8, exact-relation, endpoint, validity, and duplicate filters preserved all scored labels (Table S36), reducing concern about those specified record filters. A metadata include/exclude pass on 186 priority ligands changed no frozen class (179 include / 7 uncertain / 0 exclude) and left protein construct and mutation status unknown. Accordingly, the labels should not be treated as assay-harmonized ground truth.

Fourth, receptor replacement can raise or lower pair-level discrimination, but the experiments do not identify a molecular origin. The two receptor-sensitivity examples share PIK3CA, and the primary receptor choices are not an exhaustive receptor ensemble. Ligand protonation states, tautomers, key waters, and receptor conformations were not systematically enumerated; these omissions are especially relevant to selectivity between homologous pockets. Cognate best-of-nine redocking establishes search coverage, not necessarily correct top-ranked pose selection. Reconstructed EGFR 3POZ QC is an explicit example: a near-native pose is present among nine modes (0.760 Å) but is not ranked in the top three (top-1 9.505 Å). HER2 3RCD reconstructed QC passes at top-1 (1.855 Å).

Fifth, the primary protocol is AutoDock Vina; GNINA CNN and RTMScore rescored the same Vina poses. An independent GNINA docking search was limited to EGFR/HER2 and PIK3CA/mTOR and is not a balanced engine comparison. ECFP4 recovered much of the class structure, while close dual–selective analogue sets were too sparse for a strong local selectivity-cliff test. The study therefore cannot separate all protein-structure signal from chemical-series composition and does not include prospective testing of newly predicted dual-target compounds.

Sixth, the primary AUROCs condition on successful docking. Failures were concentrated among large, highly flexible ligands and included a boron atom-type coverage failure in the holdout. Rank-extreme sensitivity did not reverse the present pair-level conclusions, but it cannot show how such compounds would score under a method that supports them. The claimed domain is therefore the protocol-processable subset, not unrestricted drug-like chemical space.
