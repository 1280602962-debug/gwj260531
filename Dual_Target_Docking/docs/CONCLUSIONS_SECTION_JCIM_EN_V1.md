# Conclusions (JCIM Articles draft, English)

## 5. Conclusions

This study established DualFourClass-Bench to evaluate directional dual-target docking against experimentally defined A-selective and B-selective hard negatives. Across the four frozen target pairs, `summary_min` ranged from 0.430 to 0.692, and adding docking scores to ECFP4 produced only limited incremental improvement under scaffold-grouped cross-validation. Sensitivity to activity-aggregation rules did not change the main conclusions, whereas receptor replacement substantially altered apparent discrimination, including opposite-direction shifts on two PIK3CA-related pairs.

These results indicate that apparent dual-target docking discrimination is task-, chemistry-, and receptor-dependent; favorable scores in both pockets are insufficient evidence of dual activity. Dual-target virtual screening, and generative workflows that use docking as a downstream filter, should therefore consider selective hard negatives, ligand-level chemical controls, unused ligand pools, and receptor-structure sensitivity together. DualFourClass-Bench is intended to define the evidentiary boundary of docking-based dual-target recognition rather than to supply a universal docking ranking rule.
