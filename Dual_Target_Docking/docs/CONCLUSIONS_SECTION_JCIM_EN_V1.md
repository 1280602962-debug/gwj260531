# Conclusions (JCIM Articles draft, English)

## 5. Conclusions

Holding the docking score channel fixed, changing the experimental control class and its corresponding compound set substantially altered the apparent evidence for dual-target recognition in some settings. Discrimination against both-end inactives therefore does not guarantee discrimination against single-target actives. Ligand chemistry and receptor realization further bound this interpretation: a small increment after adding one docking score does not imply that all structural information is uninformative, and a stable matched-pocket advantage was not obtained. These findings are derived from eight target-pair case studies and motivate broader validation rather than establishing target-general docking performance; they do not define a target-general reliability boundary. Eight pairs are not averaged. Uncertainty remained substantial, and a failed BindingDB-native rebuild is not claimed as external validation.
