# Conclusions (JCIM Articles draft, English)

## 5. Conclusions

We established DualFourClass-Bench as an experimentally grounded evaluation setting for computational dual-target discrimination against A-selective and B-selective hard negatives. Across the four frozen target pairs, the worst-direction discrimination summary was strongly target-pair dependent (`summary_min` 0.430–0.692), and adding docking to ECFP4 produced little incremental improvement in scaffold-grouped CV AUROC. In these panels, primary estimates were largely insensitive to replacing maximum pChEMBL with the median among repeated measurements. PIK3CA/mTOR had the highest point estimate and the same directional trend in an unused ligand-pool stability check, but its uncertainty and receptor sensitivity preclude a generalizable decision rule.

Apparent dual-target docking discrimination is highly sensitive to task formulation, ligand chemical composition, and receptor realization. On EGFR/HER2, a Dual-versus-neither comparator yielded AUROC 0.756 whereas the directional worst arm was 0.430. Ligand-only references matched or exceeded docking on several pairs, and the unused-pool holdout exposed an unresolved wrong-pocket point-estimate reversal whose paired intervals included zero. Receptor realization raised or lowered apparent discrimination depending on the target pair. These findings support experimentally defined selective hard negatives, ligand-level confounder controls, panel-external stability checks, and receptor sensitivity as complementary requirements for evaluating dual-target docking. The principal contribution is an evaluation protocol rather than a universal docking method.
